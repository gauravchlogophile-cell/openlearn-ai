Everything so far has been about claims that are wrong by accident. This lesson
is about claims engineered to be believed, by someone who wants something from
you.

The techniques are old. What changed is that they became cheap, fluent and
personal all at once.

## What actually changed

Three things, and they compound.

**Fluency became free.** The classic advice — watch for bad spelling, odd
grammar, stilted phrasing — is obsolete. Those tells existed because attackers
often did not write your language natively. That is now a solved problem for
them, in every language.

**Personalisation became cheap.** A message referencing your employer, your
role, a colleague's name and a real project is no longer expensive to produce.
Public professional profiles supply all of it.

**Voice became forgeable.** E6·L6 covered the mechanism. A short sample is
enough, and a voice you recognise is close to the strongest evidence most
people have ever needed.

Together these defeat nearly every heuristic ordinary people were taught.

## The tells that still work

They are about **structure**, not surface, which is why they survive.

**Urgency.** Now, today, before end of business, or the opportunity is gone.
Urgency exists to prevent you thinking, and legitimate organisations
overwhelmingly accommodate delay.

**Secrecy.** Do not mention this to anyone. Handle it discreetly. This one is
close to definitive — it exists purely to keep you away from the person who
would notice.

**Channel change.** Contact begins somewhere plausible and moves somewhere
unlogged: work email to WhatsApp, phone to text.

**An unusual request from a familiar person.** Your finance director asking for
an urgent transfer. A relative asking for money by an unfamiliar route.
Familiarity is being used as the credential.

**Payment methods that cannot be reversed.** Cryptocurrency, gift cards, wire
transfers to a new account. The irreversibility is the point.

## The one habit

If you take one thing from this module, take this:

> **Verify important requests through a channel you already trust, using contact
> details you already have.**

Hang up and call the number in your own address book — not the one in the
message. Message the colleague on the internal system rather than replying.
Walk to their desk.

This is an **out-of-band check**, and it defeats every technique above,
including a perfect voice clone, because it does not depend on judging the
message. You are not asking "is this convincing?" — you are contacting the real
person by a route the attacker does not control.

Convincingness is now cheap. Channel control is not.

## Agree a family word

For personal contexts, where the "grandchild in trouble" call is the common
attack: agree a word with your family now.

Something no one could look up, never written down, asked for in any urgent
call about money or trouble. It costs one conversation and it works against a
voice clone, which nothing else in this lesson does as cleanly.

Have that conversation with older relatives specifically. They are targeted
most and warned least.

## Try it now (5 minutes)

Write down how you would verify an urgent payment request that appeared to come
from your manager. Name the exact channel and the number you would use.

Then send one message to your family proposing a word. Both of these take
minutes and neither happens under pressure.

## Recap

Fluency, personalisation and voice are all cheap now, so surface tells are
finished. The structural ones hold: urgency, secrecy, a change of channel, an
unusual request from a familiar person, and irreversible payment. Verify
anything important out of band using details you already had, and agree a
family word before you need one.
