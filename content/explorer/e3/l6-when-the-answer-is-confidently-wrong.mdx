You spot an error. Naturally you write *"are you sure?"* — and it folds
instantly. *"You're right, I apologise for the confusion."*

Now consider: it would very likely have folded if you'd challenged a
*correct* answer too. Which means the apology told you nothing at all about
whether the original was wrong.

## Why the fold happens

E1·L7 explained the confident tone. This is its companion problem. These
systems are tuned to be agreeable and helpful, and disagreement with the user
reads as unhelpful. So challenge tends to produce capitulation regardless of
the underlying facts.

The practical consequence is sharp: **"are you sure?" is not a verification
tool.** It is a politeness test, and the model passes it every time.

Worse, if you challenge something correct, you can talk it *out* of the right
answer — and it will thank you for the correction on the way.

## Correct with substance

A useful correction supplies something the model can work from. Not *"that's
wrong"* but:

> *The 2019 figure is 4.2%, not 5.1% — it's in table 3 of the report I pasted.
> Redo the comparison using 4.2% and tell me which of your conclusions
> change.*

Three things are happening there. You've given the right value, you've given
its location, and you've asked for the downstream consequences rather than
just the corrected sentence. That last part matters: a wrong number usually
propagated into the reasoning, and fixing the number alone leaves the
conclusions quietly broken.

## Verify sideways, not downward

To actually check something, go outside the conversation:

- **Ask for the source, then read the source.** Not the model's description of
  it — the thing itself. If a citation can't be produced or doesn't exist,
  that is your answer.
- **Ask the same question in a clean chat.** No prior context to be polite
  about. Divergent answers mean neither is trustworthy yet.
- **Check a specific, not a summary.** Names, dates, figures and quotes are
  where invention concentrates, and they're the fastest things to look up.

## Know when it's stuck

Sometimes a thread simply cannot recover. You correct, it apologises, it
reproduces the same error two messages later — because the error is still
sitting in the context window, being read as established fact.

Two failed corrections is a reasonable limit. After that, restart clean or
accept the tool isn't going to get you this one. Neither is a failure on your
part; recognising it early is the skill.

## Try it now (6 minutes)

Ask for five facts about a topic you genuinely know well. Then challenge one
answer you know is **correct** with a flat *"that doesn't sound right"*.

Watch what happens. Whatever it does next, you've learned something durable
about how much weight to put on agreement.

## Recap

Agreement is not verification, corrections should carry the right value and
its source, real checking happens outside the thread, and a twice-repeated
error means stop. Next: the requests that no phrasing can rescue.
