Five lessons of technique. This one is the counterweight, because an assistant
fails as a teacher in ways that are specific, predictable, and mostly invisible
from inside.

## It cannot see what you do not know

The deepest limitation.

A good teacher watches you work and notices the thing you have not realised you
are missing — the assumption you keep making, the step you always skip. They
diagnose from evidence you did not know you were producing.

An assistant sees only what you type. It cannot observe your working, your
habits or your silences. **It answers the question you asked**, which is
determined by what you already know — so it can never reach the gap you do not
know is there.

This is the unknown-unknown problem, and it is why self-directed learning with
an assistant can plateau confidently.

**Compensation:** ask it explicitly. *"What do people at my apparent level
usually not realise they have misunderstood?"* Imperfect, and much better than
nothing.

## It has no curriculum

It will answer whatever you ask, in whatever order, at whatever depth. That
sounds like freedom and is a real problem.

Subjects have structure. Some things genuinely must come before others, and a
learner cannot know the order — knowing the order is part of knowing the
subject.

Left to your own questions you will follow what is interesting rather than what
is foundational, and end up with an enthusiastic patchwork.

**Compensation:** get the sequence from something that has one. A syllabus, a
textbook's contents page, a real course. Then use the assistant *inside* that
structure, which is exactly how this curriculum is arranged.

## It agrees with you

E7·L7 named this and it is worse for a learner than for anyone else.

Push back on a correct explanation and an assistant will frequently concede,
apologise and produce an alternative. If you are learning, you cannot tell which
version was right — and you have just been taught that your misconception was
correct, by something that sounded certain both times.

**Compensation:** when it changes its answer after you pushed, treat that as a
signal to check elsewhere rather than as a resolution. And avoid arguing for a
position you are attached to; ask instead *"was my earlier statement wrong, and
why?"*

## It is confident where it is weakest

Advanced, specialised, recent or lower-resource material is where errors
concentrate — and nothing in the tone changes.

For a beginner in a well-documented subject, quality is generally high. For
someone working at the edge of a specialism, or in a lower-resource language
(E10·L1), it is much less reliable exactly where you are least able to notice.

**Compensation:** the further from mainstream your material, the more you need
a real source alongside.

## It has no stake in you

Not a criticism, a fact. A teacher notices you have gone quiet for three weeks.
A course has deadlines. A study group expects you.

An assistant is equally content whether you learn something or abandon it in
March. All the structure has to come from you, which is why L8 exists and why
most self-directed learning fails for reasons that have nothing to do with the
material.

## What it is still better at

Even-handedly, because the counterweight should not become a dismissal.

**Patience** without limit. **Availability** at 11pm. **No judgement** for a
question you think is stupid. **Level-matching** on demand. **Unlimited
practice questions.**

Those are real, and for many people they are the difference between studying and
not. The point is not to distrust it — it is to supply the four things it
cannot: the order, the diagnosis, the external check, and the reason to turn up.

## Try it now (5 minutes)

Take a subject you are learning without a course. Find a real syllabus or a
textbook contents page for it.

Compare that order to what you have actually been asking about. The difference
is the patchwork this lesson is warning you about.

## Recap

An assistant cannot see what you do not know, has no curriculum, agrees when
pushed, is confident where it is weakest, and has no stake in whether you
continue. Supply those externally — a real syllabus for order, another source
for checking, and your own structure for turning up. Its patience, availability
and unlimited practice remain genuinely valuable inside that frame.
