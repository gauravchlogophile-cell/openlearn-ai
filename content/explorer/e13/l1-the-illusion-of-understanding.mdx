A learning platform that never taught learning would be an odd thing, and until
this module Lrnon was one.

It starts with the problem that makes AI both an excellent study tool and a
uniquely deceptive one.

## Following is not knowing

You ask an assistant to explain something. The explanation is clear, well
structured, pitched at your level. You follow every step. You finish it feeling
that you understand.

Frequently you do not. What you have is the experience of **following an
explanation**, which is a different thing from being able to produce it,
apply it, or notice when someone gets it wrong.

This is well documented in learning research, under names like the fluency
illusion and the illusion of explanatory depth. People consistently rate their
understanding higher immediately after reading a clear account than their
performance later supports.

## Why AI makes it sharper

E7·L1 argued that fluent text disarms scepticism. The same mechanism does
something worse to learners.

An assistant produces explanations that are **maximally easy to follow** —
well organised, appropriately pitched, free of the digressions and awkwardness
of a real textbook or a real lecturer. That is genuinely valuable, and it also
maximises the gap between how well you follow and how much you retain.

Difficulty during learning is not a defect to be engineered away. A large body
of research on **desirable difficulties** finds that conditions which feel
harder — testing yourself, spacing sessions out, mixing topics — produce worse
performance during study and better performance afterwards.

So the smoothest explanation is not the best teacher, and the tool is very good
at producing smooth explanations.

## The test

There is one reliable check, and it takes two minutes:

> **Close everything. Explain it out loud, from memory, as though to someone
> else.**

Where you stall, you did not know it. Not "did not remember" — the stall *is*
the boundary of your understanding, and it is invisible while the text is in
front of you.

Most people, doing this the first time on something they just read and felt
confident about, are unpleasantly surprised. That surprise is the single most
useful thing in this module.

## What this module does

Seven lessons on using these tools in a way that survives that test:

**L2** getting explanations that are actually pitched right.
**L3** testing yourself, which is the highest-return technique available.
**L4** spacing and forgetting — why Lrnon has review cards.
**L5** teaching it back, which exposes gaps nothing else does.
**L6** where AI is a bad teacher specifically.
**L7** what cannot be learned from explanation at all.
**L8** assembling it into something you will keep doing.

## The tension worth naming now

E8·L1 said that some work has the difficulty as the point, and handing it over
gives you the artefact without the understanding.

Learning is the purest case. Every shortcut available here is a shortcut past
the thing you wanted. An assistant can make studying feel dramatically more
efficient while producing less learning, and nothing in the experience tells you
which is happening.

That is not an argument against using it. It is an argument for using it in the
specific ways the next six lessons describe, all of which deliberately make the
process harder than it needs to be.

## Try it now (5 minutes)

Take something you read or watched this week and felt you understood.

Close it. Explain it aloud from memory for one minute. Note where you stalled —
that is your actual boundary, and it is almost certainly not where you thought.

## Recap

Following a clear explanation is not the same as understanding it, and AI
produces unusually smooth explanations — which widens the gap between how well
you follow and how much you keep. Desirable difficulties feel worse and work
better. The only reliable check is explaining it from memory with everything
closed, and the stall is the finding.
