Short list. No nuance. These are the situations where the right use of this
technology is none.

## Medical emergency

**Call your emergency number.**

Chest pain or pressure. Difficulty breathing. Sudden weakness, numbness,
drooping on one side, or confused speech. Severe or uncontrolled bleeding. A
first seizure. Severe allergic reaction. A serious head injury. A baby or child
who is unresponsive, floppy, or breathing strangely.

Do not type these into anything. Minutes matter, an assistant cannot send an
ambulance, and the time spent describing it is the whole cost.

If you are not sure whether it is an emergency, that is what the non-emergency
health line from L3 is for — staffed by people who triage for a living.

## Mental health crisis

**If you are thinking about harming yourself, or you cannot keep yourself safe,
contact a crisis line or emergency services.**

Most countries have a free crisis line, available at any hour, staffed by
trained people. Find yours now and save it.

Why this is a hard stop rather than a caution:

**It cannot summon help.** If you need someone to come, it cannot arrange that.

**It cannot assess risk.** Judging how much danger somebody is in is a trained
skill built on cues a text box does not carry.

**It cannot stay.** The conversation ends when you close it. A crisis line stays
on the phone.

**It has no continuity.** No memory of you, no ability to follow up, nobody who
notices you did not call back.

An assistant may respond kindly, and kindness is not the same as help. What you
need in that moment is a person who can act, and they exist and are free.

If someone else is in danger, the same applies — emergency services, not a
search box.

## Immediate danger from another person

Domestic abuse, threats, stalking, someone at your door. **Emergency services,
and a specialist helpline afterwards.**

One specific and serious caution: if the person has access to your devices, your
searches and chat history can be seen. Use a device they cannot reach, use
private browsing, and know that many domestic abuse organisations publish
guidance on covering your tracks — including a quick-exit button on their own
sites.

This is not a hypothetical risk. Consider it before typing anything about
leaving.

## Irreversible decisions under time pressure

The pattern rather than a specific event: **something that cannot be undone,
being decided faster than you can check.**

Signing today. Transferring money now. Accepting a settlement before the
deadline this afternoon. Making a medical decision without the second opinion.

Time pressure is frequently manufactured — it was E7·L6's clearest warning sign
and E8·L7 noted that the request always arrives urgent. An assistant asked to
evaluate under pressure will produce something reasonable-sounding in seconds,
which is precisely the wrong shape of help.

The right response to *decide now* is almost always **get more time**. Ask for
an extension, say you will confirm tomorrow, tell them you are taking advice.
Legitimate parties accommodate that. The ones who do not are telling you
something.

## Do this before you need it

Two minutes, now, while nothing is wrong:

Save in your phone — **emergency number**, **non-emergency health line**, and a
**crisis line** for your country.

On some future night, looking these up will be the last thing you are capable
of. Having done it in advance is the whole point of this lesson.

## Try it now (4 minutes)

Do exactly that. Three numbers, in your contacts, now.

If you have people who might need them, send the same three to them too.

## Recap

Four hard stops: medical emergency, mental health crisis, immediate danger from
another person, and irreversible decisions under manufactured time pressure. In
each, the technology cannot do the thing that is needed — summon help, assess
risk, stay, or buy you time. Save your emergency number, non-emergency health
line and crisis line now, and if someone may have access to your devices,
consider that before typing anything.
