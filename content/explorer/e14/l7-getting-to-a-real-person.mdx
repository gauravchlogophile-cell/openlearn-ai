L1 said the reason people ask AI about health, money and law is that reaching a
professional is hard. Everything since has worked within that constraint.

This lesson attacks the constraint, because the honest end point of this module
is not better prompting. It is getting you to somebody who can actually help.

## The routes people do not know about

Availability varies by country; the categories are near-universal, and most are
underused because nobody advertises them.

**Health**
Non-emergency telephone triage lines. Pharmacists — free, walk-in, and able to
advise on a surprising range without an appointment. Nurse-led clinics. Charity
helplines for specific conditions, frequently staffed by specialist nurses.
Patient advocacy organisations.

**Money**
Free debt advice charities, genuinely independent and used to complex
situations. Government money advice services. Credit unions. Employer financial
wellbeing benefits, which almost nobody claims. Regulator comparison tools.

**Legal**
Law centres and citizens' advice organisations. University law clinics. Free
initial consultations. Trade union legal support, which covers far more than
workplace issues for many members. Ombudsmen and regulators who will examine a
case for free. Duty solicitors for some matters.

**Everything**
Local libraries frequently host or signpost free advice sessions. Community and
faith organisations often know exactly which local service handles what.

Worth finding out what exists **where you live** before you need it. That
research is much harder to do while in the middle of the problem.

## Make thirty minutes count

When you do get professional time, preparation multiplies it — and this is
where everything in the module converges.

**Arrive with the history organised** (L2). Ten minutes of your appointment
spent establishing the timeline is ten minutes not spent on your actual
question.

**Bring your questions written**, three or four, most important first. You will
not remember them otherwise.

**Bring the documents.** The letter, the contract, the test results, the
statement.

**Say what you want from the meeting** in the first minute. *"I want to
understand whether I should appeal this."* Professionals can aim at a stated
target and are frequently guessing without one.

**Take notes, or ask to record** — with consent (E4·L5). Then use L2's technique
afterwards to understand what you were told.

This is the strongest argument in the module for using these tools at all: they
turn scarce professional time into more useful professional time, which
disproportionately helps the people who get the least of it.

## Take someone with you

Underrated, free, and effective.

A second person hears what you missed, remembers what you forgot, and — this
matters — changes how some interactions go. Being accompanied makes it harder to
be dismissed.

Formal **advocacy services** exist for health, disability, mental health and
social care in many places, and are free. If you have ever left an appointment
feeling unheard, an advocate is the specific answer to that.

## When you have been dismissed

It happens, and knowing the routes helps.

Ask for the reasoning to be written down. Ask what would change the answer. Ask
for a second opinion — frequently a right rather than a favour. Use the formal
complaints process, then the ombudsman or regulator (E12·L6).

An assistant is genuinely useful here: for understanding the process, drafting
the letter, and organising your account of what happened. That is preparation
work, and it is exactly what it is good at.

## Try it now (6 minutes)

Look up one free service in your area — a law centre, a debt charity, a
non-emergency health line, or an advocacy service.

Save the contact. You are looking for it now so you do not have to look for it
later, which is the entire point.

## Recap

Free and low-cost routes to real expertise exist in every one of these domains
and are underused because nobody advertises them — find what exists where you
live before you need it. When you get professional time, arrive with the history
organised, the questions written and the documents to hand, and say what you want
in the first minute. Take someone with you. And where you have been dismissed,
ask for reasoning in writing and use the complaints route.
