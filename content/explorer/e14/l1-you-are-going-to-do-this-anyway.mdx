E7·L2 listed legal, medical and financial specifics among the things that most
need checking. E4·L1 put unverifiable high-stakes questions in the box marked
do not.

Both were right, and people ask anyway — in enormous numbers, every day,
including people who have read advice not to.

This module takes that seriously rather than repeating the instruction.

## Why the standard advice fails

"Consult a qualified professional" is correct and, for a large share of the
people reading it, not actionable.

They cannot afford one. The appointment is in eleven weeks. It is two in the
morning and they are frightened. The professional they saw did not explain
anything and they are trying to understand what was said. They live somewhere
the specialist does not.

Telling that person only "see a doctor" leaves them exactly where they were,
and they will ask an assistant anyway — just without any of the framing that
would make it safer.

**Access is the real reason people ask, and refusing to engage does not create
access.** So the useful thing is to be specific about which questions are
reasonable and which are dangerous, rather than putting the whole domain out of
bounds.

## The line that matters

Not the topic. The **kind of question**.

**Reasonable** — questions about how things work in general:

- What does this term in my diagnosis mean?
- How does an index fund differ from a managed fund?
- What is a break clause, in general?
- What questions should I ask at this appointment?
- What does this letter actually say? (E9·L2)

These are **educational**. Answering them makes you a better-informed
participant in your own situation, and being better informed is protective.

**Not reasonable** — questions that decide your specific case:

- What is wrong with me?
- Should I take this medication, at this dose?
- Should I invest in this?
- Should I sign this?
- Do I have a case?

These require facts about you the model does not have, professional
responsibility it cannot hold (E8·L4), and consequences it will not bear.

The difference is not how technical the question is. It is whether answering it
**substitutes for a decision that needs a person who is accountable**.

## Why this module exists at the end

Everything in Explorer feeds this.

E7 gave you verification, and here it is not optional. E9 covered reading
documents you cannot easily read, which is most official correspondence in these
domains. E12·L6 covered decisions made about you, which is what an insurance
refusal or a benefits decision is. E8 covered what cannot be delegated.

This module is those tools applied where the stakes are highest.

## What it will not do

Stated plainly.

This module does not give medical, legal or financial advice, and nor does the
rest of Lrnon. It will not tell you what is wrong with you or what to do with
your money.

What it will do is help you ask better questions, understand what you are told,
prepare for the conversations that matter, and recognise the small number of
situations where using AI at all is the wrong move.

## Try it now (5 minutes)

Think of a question you have asked, or wanted to ask, in one of these three
areas.

Sort it: is it about how something works in general, or does it decide your
specific case? Most people find their real question is a mixture — and
separating the two halves is what the rest of this module teaches.

## Recap

People ask about health, money and law regardless of advice not to, usually
because a professional is unaffordable, unavailable or months away. So the
useful distinction is not the topic but the question type: understanding how
something works in general is educational and protective; deciding your specific
case requires facts and accountability that no model has. This module teaches
the first and marks the second clearly.
