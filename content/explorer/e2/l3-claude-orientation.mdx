Hand three colleagues the same fifty-page report and you'll get three
different summaries — one crisp, one thorough, one oddly fixated on section
four. Assistants differ the same way, and Claude is where many people first
*feel* that difference.

## What it's typically good at

Claude, built by Anthropic, has a reputation for **long-document work** —
reading, summarising, comparing, and answering questions across large
files or several documents at once — and for **writing that reads
naturally**, with a style many find less template-like. It's a common
choice for contracts, reports, research digests, and drafting where tone
matters. Coding assistance is another frequently cited strength.

## What to keep in mind

- **Reputation ≠ guarantee.** "Known for" reflects common experience at the
  time this card was verified; model tiers on all sides move quarterly.
  Test on *your* tasks — E2's closing lesson gives you a fair method.
- **Long context is not infinite context.** Bigger windows still have
  edges, and material can still be skimmed unevenly. For a critical
  document, ask section-by-section questions rather than one giant
  "summarise everything".
- **Same machinery, same failure modes.** Long-document strength does not
  suspend E1·L7 — a fabricated detail inside an otherwise faithful summary
  is precisely the failure to watch for. Spot-check quotes against the
  page they claim.

## Try it now (5 minutes)

Take the longest document you have handy (a policy, a report, a lease).
Ask Claude: *"Summarise this in five bullet points, then list three things
a careful reader should double-check, citing the section each comes
from."* Verify one citation yourself. That verification step — not the
summary — is the professional skill.
