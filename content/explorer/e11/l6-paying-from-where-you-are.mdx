Every price in this module has been a number. For most people on earth it is
also a decision about something else they will not buy that month.

This lesson exists because a free, global course that discussed cost only in
dollars would be writing for a minority of its readers.

## The same price is not the same price

A twenty-dollar monthly subscription is a rounding error on some incomes and a
significant share of others. That is obvious and it is routinely ignored in
writing about these tools, where "just get the paid tier" is offered as though
it settles the matter.

Three things stack on top of the headline figure:

**Conversion and card fees.** Foreign-currency charges, bank markups, and a
worse exchange rate than the published one.

**Card acceptance.** Many domestic cards are declined by international
subscription services, or blocked from recurring foreign payments by the issuing
bank or by national rules on outbound payments.

**Tax added at checkout**, sometimes at rates that surprise.

The advertised price is a floor.

## Regional pricing exists, unevenly

Some providers charge less in some countries, reflecting local purchasing
power. It is inconsistent, it changes, and it is rarely advertised prominently.

**Worth doing:** check the pricing page while in your own country, without a
VPN, in your own currency. Check whether there is a local payment option — UPI
in India, local wallets and bank transfer elsewhere. Check whether a regional
reseller offers it.

**Also worth checking:** student and educator pricing, which several providers
offer and which is frequently a large discount rather than a token one. And
whether a national or institutional programme covers it — some universities and
governments have arrangements.

## What not to do

Stated plainly because it is common advice online and it is bad advice.

**Do not misrepresent your country** to obtain a lower price. It breaches the
terms you agree to, and the usual consequence is not a warning but the loss of
the account, including anything you had stored in it.

**Do not use someone else's payment method** without their genuine, informed
agreement — and understand that shared-account arrangements usually breach terms
too.

The risk is asymmetric. You save a modest monthly amount and stake an account
you may depend on.

## When you cannot pay at all

A real situation for a lot of people, and there are legitimate routes.

**Free tiers**, per L5, cover more than most assume.

**Local models**, per L5, cost nothing recurring and need only a capable enough
device.

**Regional providers.** Several countries have domestic AI services priced in
local currency with local payment methods, and for work in the local language
they are sometimes better than the global option — E10·L1 noted that
region-specific models often outperform general ones on their target languages.

**Institutional access.** Your university, library, employer or a government
skilling programme. Ask before assuming.

**Shared organisational accounts**, where the organisation licenses it properly
and you are a named user. This is legitimate; informal password-sharing is not.

## For anyone building something

If you are building on the Practitioner track and your users are in a range of
countries, this lesson is about them rather than you.

A per-seat price that is trivial in one market is prohibitive in another. Usage
that costs cents adds up differently against different incomes. If your product
charges, decide deliberately whether you price by market — and if you cannot,
know who that excludes rather than discovering it later.

## Try it now (5 minutes)

Open the pricing page of a tool you use, in your own country and currency, with
no VPN.

Check for a local payment method, a student rate, and the actual total after tax
and card fees. That number, not the headline, is what it costs you.

## Recap

The headline price is a floor — conversion fees, card acceptance and tax sit on
top, and the same figure is a very different decision on different incomes.
Check regional, student and institutional pricing, and local payment methods,
from your own country. Never misrepresent where you are: the saving is small and
the account is the stake. Free tiers, local models and regional providers are the
legitimate routes when paying is not possible.
