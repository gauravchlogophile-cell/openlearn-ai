Reading is not one skill and it fails in several different ways. Dense text is
hard when you have dyslexia, when your vision is limited, when you are exhausted
or in pain, when you are reading a second language, and when the writing is
simply bad.

What these have in common is that **the words are not the problem — the shape
they arrive in is**. And shape is something AI is genuinely good at changing.

## Ask for a different shape, not a summary

There is an important distinction here, and E4·L3 covered half of it.

A **summary** is shorter and leaves things out. A **reshaping** keeps everything
and changes how it is presented. For a document you actually need — a letter
from a landlord, a medical leaflet, a contract — you usually want the second.

> *Rewrite the text below so it is easier to read. Keep every fact, condition
> and exception. Do not shorten it if that means leaving something out.*
>
> *Use short sentences. One idea per sentence. Everyday words instead of formal
> ones. Put the thing I have to do first, then the details.*
>
> *Then list separately: anything I must do, and any deadline.*

That final instruction is doing real work. In official letters the action and
the deadline are frequently buried in the fourth paragraph, and they are the
part that matters.

## The one check that matters

Simplification drops qualifiers. E5·L7 established this and it is more
important here, because the whole point is that you may not be re-reading the
original closely.

> *"You may be eligible for a reduction if you have been resident for six
> months and are not claiming elsewhere."*

becomes

> *"You can get a reduction."*

Shorter, clearer, and now wrong for most readers.

So add one line to every simplification request:

> *Then list every condition, exception or "only if" from the original, in
> plain words.*

Read that list. It is short, it is where the meaning lives, and checking it is
far less work than reading the whole original.

## Hearing it and seeing it together

Text-to-speech is the oldest tool here and still among the best. Two things
worth knowing.

**Following along helps.** Many people read better hearing the words while
seeing them than doing either alone. Lrnon's read-aloud is built this way
deliberately: it does not replace the page, it reads the page you are looking
at.

**Speed is a setting.** Most people settle well above the default once used to
it, and a faster voice makes long documents realistic rather than punishing.

## Fonts, spacing and contrast come first

Before any AI is involved: change how text looks. It is free, instant and
frequently sufficient.

Larger text, more line spacing, shorter lines, higher contrast, and a typeface
with distinguishable letterforms all measurably help. Lrnon's accessibility
panel does all five, and every major browser and operating system has
equivalents.

Try that first. Reshaping with AI is for documents you cannot restyle — a PDF,
a photographed letter, someone else's website.

## Photographs of text

A great deal of important text arrives as an image: a letter photographed on a
phone, a screenshot, a form.

Most assistants can now read these. Two cautions specific to this case:

**Numbers and dates are where it errs.** A misread digit in a reference number
or an amount is both easy to produce and hard to notice.

**Ask for the text first, then the explanation.** *"Type out exactly what this
says, then explain it in plain words."* The transcription is checkable by
someone else in a way an explanation is not, and it gives you something to
quote when you ring them up.

## Try it now (7 minutes)

Take a genuinely dense document — an official letter, terms and conditions, a
policy.

Ask for the reshaped version, the list of actions and deadlines, and the list
of conditions. Read the conditions list. That is the part that would have been
lost.

## Recap

Change how text looks before reaching for anything cleverer — size, spacing,
line length, contrast and typeface are free and often enough. When you do use
AI, ask for a reshaping that keeps every fact rather than a summary, and always
ask separately for the conditions and exceptions, because those are what
simplification drops. For photographed text, get the exact words first.
