Point a camera at something and ask what it is. For blind and low-vision users
this has genuinely changed what can be done alone — reading post, checking
whether the milk has turned, finding the right door, knowing what is in a
photograph a friend sent.

It is also the use in this module where being confidently wrong does the most
damage, because there is no glance available to catch it.

## Ask for what you need, not for a description

"Describe this image" gets you a paragraph of scene-setting, most of which is
not what you wanted.

Ask the actual question:

> *What does the sign say, word for word?*
> *Is there anyone in this room?*
> *What is the expiry date on this packet?*
> *Which of these two boxes is the blue one?*
> *Is the cooker ring on?*

Specific questions get shorter, more accurate answers, and they let you tell
when the tool has not understood — a non-answer to a specific question is
obvious in a way that a vague paragraph is not.

Ask for a general description when you actually want one — a photo of family, a
view — where there is no single fact you are after.

## Ask it to say when it cannot tell

The most valuable instruction in this lesson:

> *If you cannot tell, say so. Do not guess.*

Without it you get a plausible answer to everything. With it you get "the image
is too blurred to read the third line", which is genuinely useful — you can
retake the photo.

Follow it up when precision matters:

> *How confident are you about the number? Would a clearer photo help?*

## The failure modes

Know these specifically, because you cannot spot them by looking.

**Misread digits.** Numbers are where this most often goes wrong — expiry
dates, doses, reference numbers, prices, house numbers. A 3 read as an 8 arrives
with exactly the same confidence as a correct reading.

**Invented plausible detail.** Asked what is on a table, a model may add things
that are usually on tables. The description reads naturally and contains
something that is not there.

**Text that is nearly right.** Long text gets transcribed with small
substitutions — a word swapped for a similar one, a line skipped. Fine for
gist, not fine for instructions.

**Stale scene.** The photo is a moment. "The door is closed" was true when the
shutter fired.

**Confident answers about people.** Age, mood, relationship, whether someone
looks unwell — treat all of these as guesses, because that is what they are.

## Where it must not be the only source

Draw this line before you need it:

**Medication.** Never identify a tablet or a dose from a photo alone. Use a
pharmacist, a labelling system, or a dedicated medication-identification
service.

**Anything you are signing.** Get a human or an official accessible copy.
Organisations are usually obliged to provide one, and asking costs a phone
call.

**Money.** Note denominations, card details, meter readings — verify by another
route.

**Safety.** Whether an appliance is off, whether a substance is what the bottle
says, whether a road is clear.

This is not because the tool is bad. It is because "I could not check" is
different from "I checked", and these are the cases where that difference is
expensive.

## Use the purpose-built ones too

General assistants are convenient. Dedicated tools exist for this exact job —
some with the option to connect to a sighted human volunteer or a trained agent
when the machine cannot tell.

That combination is the practical answer for anything important: try the fast
automated route, escalate to a person when it matters. There is no prize for
refusing help.

## Try it now (7 minutes)

Photograph something with small print — a packet, a bill, a label.

Ask a specific question about a number on it, adding *say so if you cannot
tell*. Then check the answer against the real thing, or ask someone. That
comparison tells you how much to trust this for your own use, which is worth
more than any general claim.

## Recap

Ask the specific question rather than for a description, and always add "say so
if you cannot tell". Know that digits, invented detail and near-right
transcription are the failure modes, and that none announce themselves. For
medication, signatures, money and safety, use a second route — and use the
purpose-built tools, including the ones that can reach a human.
