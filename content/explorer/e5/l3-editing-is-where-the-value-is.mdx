Most people point assistants at writing they have not done. Point one at
writing you *have* done and the whole risk profile inverts.

You wrote it, so you know whether a criticism lands. Checking costs seconds.
By E4·L1's test this is close to the ideal case, and it is the use most likely
to make you better at the job rather than just faster.

## Ask for objections, not an assessment

"What do you think of this?" produces praise followed by three mild
suggestions. It is the least useful question available.

Ask for something specific and adversarial:

> *Below is a draft. You are a sceptical reader who disagrees with its
> conclusion.*
>
> *Give me the five strongest objections. For each: quote the sentence that
> invites it, state the objection in one line, and say what would answer it.*
>
> *Do not comment on style. Do not tell me what works.*

Forbidding praise matters. Left open, a model will balance criticism with
reassurance, and the reassurance dilutes the part you needed.

## The question you cannot ask yourself

Here is the one that earns its place permanently:

> *Where will a reader misunderstand this? Quote the sentence and say what
> they will think it means.*

You cannot answer this about your own writing. You know what you meant, so you
read your meaning into your sentences automatically. That is not carelessness —
it is a structural blind spot, and it is the thing a fresh reader is for.

An assistant is not a fresh reader in any deep sense, but it *is* a system
producing the most likely reading of your words, which is exactly the
approximation you want.

## Reader-specific critique

Generic critique is generic. Name the reader:

> *Critique this as: a finance director who will only read the first
> paragraph and the numbers.*
>
> *Critique this as: someone who was in the meeting and disagreed.*

The second is uncomfortable and useful. Documents circulate to people whose
objections you have been avoiding, and it is better to meet them at your desk.

## Take the objections, not the rewrites

When a critique lands, resist asking for a fixed version. Fix it yourself.

Rewriting is where your judgement is, and it is also where the understanding
gets built. Hand that back and you are in E4·L8's third category — the artefact
improves and you do not. Use the criticism; keep the pen.

## Try it now (7 minutes)

Take something you have already written. Ask for the five strongest objections
with quoted sentences and no style comments.

Then ask where a reader will misunderstand it. Fix the one that made you wince,
in your own words.

## Recap

Point the assistant at writing you have already done. Ask for the strongest
objections with quoted sentences, forbid praise, name the specific reader, and
ask where you will be misunderstood. Then do the rewriting yourself — the
criticism is the product, not the replacement text.
