This is the last lesson of the Explorer track.

Sixty-three lessons have given you capability and a lot of individual
judgements. This one collects the judgements into something you can actually
consult — because the moment you need them, you will be busy, under pressure,
and wanting a particular answer.

## Why in advance

You will not reason well about this in the moment. Nobody does.

At the point of decision there is a deadline, someone waiting, and a strong
preference for the convenient answer — the exact conditions E7·L7 described,
where you scrutinise what you dislike and wave through what suits you.

A code written calmly is you, deciding, at your best. Consulting it later is not
outsourcing the judgement; it is honouring one you already made under better
conditions.

## Eight questions

Answer these. Specifically, in your own words.

**1. What will I always disclose?** (E8·L2) Which work, to whom, in what
words.

**2. What will I never paste?** (E4·L8) The categories, plus what you do
instead when the task is real but the material is sensitive.

**3. What decisions stay mine?** (E4·L8, E8·L4) Where you will not let output
decide, particularly about people.

**4. What do I always verify?** (E7·L8) Your three standing rules — at minimum,
never repeat an unopened citation.

**5. What will I not generate?** (E6·L8) The likeness rules, the four
categories, your line on real people.

**6. Whose work does mine affect, and how will I involve them?** (E8·L6)

**7. What will I refuse, whoever asks?** (E8·L7) The floor. The items that do
not move when the person asking can make life difficult.

**8. When will I review this?** A date.

Eight answers. One page. If it runs longer, it is a document you will not
consult.

## Make it about actions

Values are easy to write and impossible to apply. *"I will use AI
responsibly"* commits you to nothing and settles no argument at three in the
afternoon.

Write behaviours instead:

> *I will not put a citation in anything I publish unless I have opened it.*
>
> *I will not paste customer data into any assistant, including summaries of
> it. If I need the analysis, I will strip identifiers first.*
>
> *I will tell clients when a substantial part of a deliverable was
> AI-assisted, in one specific sentence.*
>
> *I will not generate images of real people for anything published.*

Each of those tells you what to do in a specific situation. That is the test —
if a line cannot settle an actual decision, rewrite it until it can.

## Set the review date

Six months. Put it in the calendar now, or it will not happen.

Tools change, norms shift, and your work changes. Some rules will look
over-cautious and some will look naive. Both are worth catching, and an
unreviewed code has two failure modes: quietly ignored, or obeyed past the point
it made sense.

## What you have

Across Explorer you have covered what these systems are and are not, the
assistants and how to choose, how to brief them, where they fit in a working
day, how to produce documents that hold up, how to make and label media, how to
verify claims and resist manipulation, and now where your own lines are.

The last one is what makes the rest safe to use. Capability without it is what
E7·L8 warned about: a more efficient source of confident errors.

If you are continuing, the Practitioner track builds on all of this. If you are
not, you have what you need — and the code you just wrote is the part that
keeps mattering.

## Try it now (8 minutes)

Answer the eight questions. Not perfectly — writing something you will revise
beats planning to write something better.

Then put the review date in your calendar, and tell one person what is in it.
Saying it aloud is what turns a document into a commitment.

## Recap

Answer eight questions — disclosure, what you will not paste, which decisions
stay yours, what you always verify, what you will not generate, whose work
yours affects, what you refuse whoever asks, and when you will review it.
Write behaviours rather than values, keep it to a page, and set the date. That
completes the Explorer track.
