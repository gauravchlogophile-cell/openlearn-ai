The last lesson of the track is a single page. It is the page you write before you build
anything, and it pulls the earlier modules into one place where you can read them together.

The point of writing it is not paperwork. It is that a decision you cannot write down, you
probably cannot defend, and a decision you cannot defend should not be made yet.

## The page

One page, written in plain words, with these parts.

### 1. What this is for

One sentence on the problem, in the words of the people who have it (P9·L1). Then one on what
success looks like, in observable terms (P9·L2).

### 2. What we considered

A short list of alternatives, and **doing nothing must be on it**.

> A document or template (P4·L8).
> A script with no model in it (P5·L2).
> A workflow with one model step (P6·L1).
> Making the process simpler (P11·L2).
> Doing nothing.

For each, a line on why it was or was not chosen. If "do nothing" is not clearly worse, say
so honestly. Often the process is tolerable and the build would cost more than it saves.

### 3. What it touches

The data (P8·L1): what personal data, where it goes, which plan the provider is on, how
long each copy is kept. The accounts and permissions (P5·L3). The people on the receiving
end (L5).

### 4. What could go wrong

The worst single call of each tool (P6·L3), the cost on a bad day (P7·L1), the quiet
failures (P5·L5), what injection could do (P3·L4), and what a wrong output costs the person
affected.

### 5. Who answers for it

One named person, with authority and a backup (L4). And how an affected person reaches a
human.

### 6. How it stops

The control, who may press it, when it was tested while running, and the manual path behind it
(L6).

### 7. What it depends on

The vendors, the exit plan and the triggers for using it (P10).

### 8. When we will look again

A date. Six months is a reasonable start. On that date: is it still needed, still correct, still
used, and still the right size?

## Why do it before building

It changes decisions.

The act of writing "what could go wrong" often reveals that the answer is "something we cannot
repair", and that moves the task into L1's territory. Writing "doing nothing" occasionally
shows that the problem was smaller than the solution.

And a page written before the build is honest in a way one written afterwards is not. After
the work is done, the rationale drifts to justify what was built.

## The checklist behind it

The track can be read as a set of questions, each answered on the page.

> **P1 and P2:** is the prompt, and the grounding, as good as it can be?
> **P3:** what happens when the text it reads is hostile?
> **P4:** is the configuration owned, tested and portable?
> **P5 and P6:** is the chain or the agent bounded, and is the person where the damage is?
> **P7:** what does it cost, and how would we know it was failing?
> **P8:** where does the data go and are we entitled to send it?
> **P9:** will anyone use it, and have we told them the truth about it?
> **P10:** what happens when a vendor changes?
> **P11:** should this be automated at all?

You will not need every question for every task. The point is that having asked them, you
know which ones you skipped.

## The right amount

The ending of the track is the same sentence as the beginning of this module, said more
carefully.

**The right amount of automation is often less than is possible.** Not because the tools are
poor, but because every automation is a commitment: to own it, keep it true, watch it, answer
for it, and to retire it properly. Commitments should be made where the return is worth it.

That judgement, about what to build and what to leave, is the skill this whole track has been
approaching from different sides. It is also the one that does not age when the tools change.

## A page from Lrnon

The project's own record has several of these pages in embryo. The commit messages explain
the reasoning behind each change, including what was wrong and what was corrected. The code
comments record why a thing is the way it is. The guides state what is built, what is
switched off and why.

What it lacks is the one page for the assistant in the form above, written before it was
built. If it had existed, several things this track has taken as lessons would have been
asked in advance: the plan and what it implies for what learners are told, the reviewer who
would do the reading, the failure the console could not see, and the gate that was enforced
only in the interface.

That is the use of the page. Not that you get it all right, but that you ask early and cheaply
what was asked late and expensively here.

## Try it now (45 minutes)

Choose an automation you have built, or one you are considering. Write the page: eight
headings, a few lines each.

Give it to one person who would be affected and one who would maintain it. Ask each what is
missing.

If writing it makes you want to change the plan, it has already done its job.

## Recap

End with one page written before you build: what it is for, the alternatives including doing
nothing, what it touches, what could go wrong, who answers for it, how it stops, what it
depends on, and when you will look again. Writing it first changes decisions, because the
rationale otherwise drifts to justify what was built. The earlier modules are the questions
behind it. The right amount of automation is often less than is possible, because every
automation is a commitment, and judging which ones are worth making is the skill that outlasts
the tools.
