Most platforms let you attach files to a custom assistant. This is P2 with the
dials hidden: the uploads are cut up, indexed and retrieved by machinery you do
not see and cannot tune.

That is a reasonable trade. It also means the failure modes from P2 are still
present, and you have fewer instruments to notice them with.

## What the platform decided for you

P2 made you choose four things. A platform chooses all four and does not say
which way:

**How the file was cut up.** P2·L2 argued a chunk must still mean something on
its own. A table split from its headings, or a policy split from the section
number it refers to, retrieves perfectly and informs nobody.

**How passages are ranked.** Keyword, meaning-based, or a mixture. You cannot
see the scores, so P2·L3's reasoning about rare words carrying the weight is
invisible to you here.

**How many passages reach the model.** Usually a handful. Which handful, you
cannot inspect.

**What happens when retrieval finds nothing.** The important one. Some platforms
answer from general knowledge without saying so, which is the behaviour P2·L7
spends a whole lesson arguing against. An answer that looks grounded and is not
is worse than a refusal, because it carries the authority of your attached
document without its content.

You can find out which of those last two your platform does, and it takes about
five minutes. It is the single most useful thing to know about a tool you are
about to hand to colleagues.

## The failure is age, not error

Attached knowledge rarely goes wrong by being incorrect. It goes wrong by being
**old**, and this is the one problem a custom assistant makes worse rather than
better.

The shape is always the same. Someone builds an assistant, uploads the policy,
and it works. The policy changes. Nobody thinks of the assistant, because it is
not in anybody's list of places the policy lives. Six months later a colleague
asks it a question and receives a confident, well-formatted answer from the
superseded version — with no indication that it is a year behind.

A pasted prompt does not do this. You paste the current document because you
have it open. A saved configuration keeps whatever it was given, indefinitely,
and nothing about the answer reveals its age.

## Make the age visible

Three things, in order of how much they buy:

**Put the date in the file.** Not in the filename — in the text, at the top:
*"Refund policy, current as of 14 October 2026. Owner: finance."* Then the
assistant can be told to quote it, and the answer carries its own age.

**Tell it to say what it read.** The citation requirement from P2·L5, applied
here: an answer naming the document and its date lets a reader judge. One that
says "our policy is 14 days" does not.

**Give the knowledge an owner and a review date.** Not a platform feature — a
line in whatever document governs that policy, saying it also lives in an
assistant. The assistant cannot remind you. The policy's owner can.

That third one is the only durable fix, and it is organisational rather than
technical. Lrnon's version is a build-time check: a tool description older than
120 days fails the build, so staleness stops a deploy rather than waiting to be
noticed. Most teams cannot wire that into an uploaded file, which is exactly why
the date has to be written where the answer will carry it.

## Fewer, better files

The instinct is to upload everything available, on the reasoning that more
context cannot hurt. It can, in two ways.

**Ranking gets harder.** P2·L5 made this point about passages and it holds for
documents: a relevant file among twenty marginal ones is harder to surface, not
easier.

**Contradictions become invisible.** Two documents disagreeing is the worst case
for attached knowledge, because the model will answer from one of them without
mentioning the other. Three versions of a policy in a folder is not redundancy,
it is a coin toss with a confident voice.

Upload the current version. Delete the superseded ones, rather than keeping them
"for reference" — a folder is reference, an assistant's knowledge base is an
answer.

## Try it now (10 minutes)

Take an assistant with files attached, or build one with a single document, and
ask it two questions.

1. Something the document clearly answers. Does the reply name the document?
2. **Something adjacent that the document does not cover at all.** Watch
   carefully. Does it say the document does not cover it, or does it answer
   anyway?

The second question is the one worth your time. If it answered, your assistant
is not grounded in the way you assumed — it is a general assistant with some
reading material, and every answer it gives needs the scepticism you would apply
to an ungrounded one.

## Recap

Attaching files is P2's retrieval with the dials hidden, so find out the one
thing that matters most — whether your platform answers from general knowledge
when retrieval finds nothing. The usual failure is age rather than error, and a
saved configuration holds whatever it was given indefinitely. Put the date and
owner inside the file so the answer can carry them, give the knowledge a human
owner, and upload the current version only.
