L1 said grounding fixes currency. That is true of the model's training and
false of your index — you have moved the staleness problem, not removed it. Now
it is yours to manage, which is an improvement only if you actually manage it.

## The failure that matters most is deletion

Updates are the obvious case: a document changes, the index holds the old text,
answers lag. Bad, and usually noticed.

Deletion is worse and quieter.

> A policy is withdrawn because it was wrong. The file is removed. The index
> still holds its passages.

The system now answers confidently from guidance the organisation has formally
retracted. Nobody sees a stale document — they see an answer. There is no
visible artefact to notice, and the more authoritative your interface looks,
the longer this survives.

**Removing a document must remove its passages.** If your pipeline only ever
adds, you have built something that cannot forget, and the first time it
matters will be the worst time.

## Rebuild or update

**Rebuild everything** on a schedule. Simple, hard to get wrong, and it handles
deletion for free because the new index only contains what currently exists.
Fine while a rebuild takes minutes.

**Update incrementally** as documents change. Necessary at scale, and it
demands that you handle removal explicitly — which is exactly the case that
gets forgotten, because nothing fails loudly when you skip it.

Start with rebuilds. Move to incremental only when the rebuild genuinely
becomes too slow, and write the deletion path first when you do.

## Carry the date through

Store when each passage was indexed, and show it.

> *"Refunds are issued within 14 days — Returns policy, section 4, indexed 12
> March."*

That date converts an unanswerable question ("is this current?") into an
instant judgement. A reader who knows the policy changed in April now knows to
check. Without the date they have no way to even suspect.

This is Explorer E7's habit applied to your own systems: name what you checked
and when. Lrnon does it in two places — every registry card carries a
`lastVerified` date, and a build **fails** when a tool description is more than
120 days old. Not a reminder; a failure.

## Decide what "too old" means before it matters

Different material ages differently. A statutory notice may hold for years; a
price list is wrong within a week.

Pick a threshold per source and act on it — refuse, warn, or flag for review.
Deciding in advance is much easier than deciding while looking at a specific
embarrassing answer, when the temptation to rationalise is strongest.

## Try it now (6 minutes)

For the documents behind your grounded system, answer three questions in
writing:

1. When a document is **deleted**, what removes its passages? If the answer is
   "nothing", that is today's most important bug.
2. How old can a passage get before an answer from it is a problem?
3. Does anything currently show a reader when the source was last checked?

## Recap

Grounding moves staleness from the model's training into your index, where it
becomes your responsibility. Deletion is the failure that hides best — make
sure removing a document removes its passages. Rebuild wholesale while you can.
Carry the indexed date through to the reader, and decide what "too old" means
before you are looking at a specific bad answer.
