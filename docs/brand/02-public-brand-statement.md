# Brand guide 2 of 4 — Public brand statement

**Audience:** learners, visitors, press, anyone assessing whether to trust us.
**Status:** publishable as written. `/about` is the live implementation.

## Positioning

> **Lrnon teaches people to use AI well — free for every learner, and without
> anyone paying us to recommend anything.**

One line beneath it, where more room exists:

> We name real tools, show you where every description came from and when we
> checked it, and teach you how to decide for yourself.

## What Lrnon is

A free, open curriculum for using AI competently and safely. Fourteen Explorer
modules covering what these systems are, how to ask them things, where they fit
in ordinary work, how to verify what they tell you, and where not to use them
at all — plus tracks for people who want to build with them.

No account required to learn. No paywalls. Content under CC BY-SA, code under
MIT. Sky, a friendly study buddy, is on every page to suggest a next step and show
your own progress; it is built to be calm, and to work for people with dyslexia
or sensitivity to light and movement.

## What Lrnon is not

- Not a vendor, a reseller, or an affiliate of one.
- Not an AI product. We teach about AI; we are a website with lessons.
- Not a certification body. Certification is now built — the schema, the
  assessment, the published rubric and the public verification page all exist —
  but it is switched off, and no learner can obtain one yet. It opens when two
  reviewers have agreed on twenty sample answers and the guardian-email path has
  been tested with real families. Even once it opens, a Lrnon Certificate
  records work we assessed against our own published standard. It does not
  replace a school certificate or a board result, and it says so on its face.
- Not a replacement for professional advice on health, money or law.

## The five commitments

**Nobody pays to be in a lesson.** No vendor pays for placement, ranking or
inclusion in a lesson, a tool card or a comparison. No affiliate links, no paid
reviews. Lrnon takes donations, grants and, under strict published rules,
advertising, and none of it may touch what is taught. A learner never pays and
nothing is behind money. Until 15 October 2026 this commitment read "No
sponsorship, ever" and covered all money; the change was published on `/support`
the same day.

**Neutral, not silent.** We name real products, because teaching about AI
without them would be useless. Naming is not endorsing.

**Sourced and dated.** Every tool named in a lesson shows a link to the
vendor's own material and the date we last checked it.

**The vendor is the authority, not us.** Where our description and their
current documentation disagree, theirs is right and ours is stale — which is
why the link is always there.

**We say what we do not know.** Where a question is unsettled, the lessons say
so and say what would settle it.

## Tone of voice

**Plain.** Short sentences. Ordinary words. If a term needs defining, define it
once and move on.

**Specific.** "Checked on 22 August 2026" rather than "regularly updated".
Numbers and dates, not adjectives.

**Calm.** No hype, no urgency, no exclamation marks. The style guide already
lints out *revolutionary*, *game-changing*, *mind-blowing* and *superpower*.

**Honest about limits.** Naming what we do not cover is a feature. Readers
trust a source that declines more than one that never does.

**Never condescending.** Learners are adults with limited time, not beginners
to be flattered.

## Proof points, for anyone checking

- Content lint, unit tests, typecheck and a full database test suite run on
  every change.
- A tool description older than 120 days fails the build.
- Every lesson ends with questions; completion requires answering them.
- The site works offline, at 320px, with a screen reader, and with JavaScript
  doing very little.
- Community features stay closed until a named safeguarding owner and deputy
  exist — because a room nobody is watching is worse than no room.
