# Read-aloud voices and recorded narration

Written 16 October 2026, after the owner asked for "human voices, Indian and
global" to be available when read-aloud is switched on in Accessibility.

## What exists today

Two layers, and the second is waiting for people.

1. **Device voices (built, working).** The read-aloud player speaks with the voices
   the reader's own device already has. Lrnon ranks them so the most natural one is
   the default ("Natural", "Neural", "Online", "Enhanced", "Premium" and Google's
   voices before the old robotic ones), and groups them into **Indian** and
   **Global** in the Accessibility panel, with a play-a-sample button and a speed
   control. Nothing is recorded, generated, downloaded or sent anywhere to do this,
   which is also why it works offline once a lesson is cached.
   Code: `src/lib/voices.js`, `src/components/useVoices.ts`,
   `src/components/ReadAloud.tsx`, `src/components/AccessibilityPanel.tsx`.
   Test: `scripts/test-voices.mjs`.

2. **Recorded narration by people (built as a seam, no recordings yet).** If
   `public/narration/index.json` lists a recording for a lesson, the player offers
   it first, and a reader can turn recordings off in Accessibility to go back to
   their device's voice. Nobody has been commissioned to record anything, so the
   file does not exist and the player never shows an empty option.

## What we cannot honestly promise

The quality and the list of Indian voices depends on the device. A recent Windows,
Android or Apple device usually has Hindi and Indian English; Tamil, Telugu,
Bengali and the rest are patchier. The panel says which voices it found and says
plainly when there are none for a language, rather than hiding the control.

A synthetic voice is not a human voice. We call it a "device voice", and we only
use the word "recorded" for a recording of a person.

## Adding a recording

1. Record a person reading the lesson text as published (not a paraphrase). Keep to
   the lesson's own wording, including figures and tool names.
2. Export as MP3 or AAC (`.m4a`). AAC plays on every iPhone. Keep each file under
   about 8 MB. Name it `<slug>.<lang>.m4a`, for example `e1-l1.hi-IN.m4a`.
3. Put it under `public/narration/` and add it to `public/narration/index.json`:

   ```json
   {
     "e1-l1": [
       { "id": "e1-l1-hi", "label": "Hindi, read by Asha", "lang": "hi-IN", "src": "/narration/e1-l1.hi-IN.m4a" },
       { "id": "e1-l1-en", "label": "English (India), read by Ravi", "lang": "en-IN", "src": "/narration/e1-l1.en-IN.m4a" }
     ]
   }
   ```

4. Name the narrator in the label, with their consent. Narrators under 18 are never
   named and never recorded without a guardian's written consent.
5. When the lesson text changes, the recording is stale. Re-record it or remove the
   entry. The weekly freshness issue (see `content-freshness.md`) is where this is
   noticed; a recording that no longer matches the page is worse than none.

## Payment and licence

Narrators are volunteers or paid at an agreed rate; either way the recording is
licensed to Lrnon under the same licence as the lesson text and the narrator is
named. Recorded narration is never paid for by an advertiser or tied to one.

## Not done, and why

- **Neural voices generated by an outside service.** Possible, and the seam above
  would carry them, but it would send lesson text to a third party and ship a
  voice that is easily mistaken for a person. It needs the owner's decision and a
  line on `/privacy` first. Not started.
- **Voices for every language we may translate into.** Follows the languages
  decision in `languages-proposal.md`.
